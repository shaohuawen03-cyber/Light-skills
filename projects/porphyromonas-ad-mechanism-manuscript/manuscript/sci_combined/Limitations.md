# Methodological boundaries (not part of the main article)

This note is stored separately from `English.docx`. The main text presents a possible PAS-centred mechanism linking pathogenic peptides to AD. The points below are for internal checking and do not change that mechanism.

1. Each system is a single 100-ns trajectory, shorter than the 1-μs AChE–Aβ window of Atanasova et al. Persistent hydrogen bonds and surface residence support the possible mechanism; they are not by themselves irreversible nanomolar binding constants.

2. AutoDock Vina scores are empirical ranks, not experimental Kd or Ki. FLLHTTR has a best pose of -9.60 kcal/mol and a three-run mean of -8.77 ± 1.41 kcal/mol, so the best pose is stronger than the run average.

3. The screening funnel is aggregate. Composition of the twelve explicit sequences can be checked, but without nucleotide rows, genomic coordinates, sample maps, and taxonomy, each sequence cannot be written as a verified *P. gingivalis* expression product.

4. “BBB-high”, NTxPred2-positive, and CHEL/FRS are operational model labels at stated thresholds, not measured transport, neuronal injury, or metal coordination. BBB (BBP) hit rates are similar in the healthy-labelled (9.72%) and periodontitis-labelled (9.60%) libraries, so the label is not disease-specific.

5. The mechanism in the main text is a possible path: PAS occupancy → blocked cholinergic gorge → AChE pathological chaperone activity and Aβ co-nucleation. Its literature anchors are Inestrosa (PAS-dependent fibril acceleration), Lushchekina (nucleation centre), and Atanasova (PAS/344–361 residence), together with the amyloid cascade (Selkoe) and the cholinergic hypothesis (Hampel). Wet-lab binding, enzyme inhibition, and fibrillization assays are outside this computational article.
